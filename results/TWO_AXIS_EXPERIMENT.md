# The two-axis experiment — trace validity × answer correctness

Branch `exp/answer-correctness`. Generated by `tests/audit/two_axis.py`
from committed artifacts. No Lean re-run, no GPU, no network.

**Status: Phase 4 pilot complete and reported here. The full trajectory-level run
is not in this document** — it needs a generation run that does not fit on this
machine. See §7 and `results/RUN_PLAN_200.md`.

---

## 0. Read this before the grid: only one of the two axes is ours

**The answer axis is a property of the dataset, not of our pipeline.**

A trajectory's final answer is read from FormalStep's own `previous_steps`. It
was written by whatever model the Safe pipeline used to produce the
chain-of-thought — the supervisor identifies it as Llama-3.1; the released
dataset card does not name a generator, and nothing here depends on which model
it was, only on the fact that **it is not ours**.

Consequently:

> **Goedel-Prover cannot move the answer axis. Neither can temperature,
> best-of-n, error-feedback repair, a different prover, a better prompt, or any
> other intervention this project can make.**

What the grid actually cross-tabulates is:

| axis | question | whose behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| horizontal | did the CoT model's trajectory reach the right final answer? | **the dataset's** — fixed, immovable |
| vertical | can Goedel-Prover prove that trajectory's formalised step(s)? | **ours** — responds to our interventions |

This is a real and useful cross-tab: it asks whether our verifier's verdict
tracks the correctness of somebody else's reasoning, which is exactly the
question "is step-level verification a useful signal?". But a reader who assumes
both axes respond to our interventions will draw wrong conclusions from it —
above all from §5, where the answer axis is invariant across temperature **by
construction** rather than as an empirical null.

Two direct consequences, stated here rather than buried:

1. **Any future prover improvement moves the vertical axis only.** Cell b can be
   shrunk by better formalisation or a better prover; cell c cannot be shrunk by
   anything we do to the prover, because the answers in it are already correct.
2. **A "wrong answer" in this study is never our model's error.** It is the CoT
   model's. Our pipeline's only error mode here is failing to prove a step.

---

## 1. What the unit of analysis is, and whether it clusters

**Unit: one trajectory, n = 50.** In this pilot the rows **do not cluster**.

FormalStep's `previous_steps` field is the full step list of the trajectory a row
belongs to, repeated on every row of that trajectory — not a growing prefix.
Grouping on it yields **2,459 trajectories across 500 problems** (~5 per
problem), with the reconstruction exact: 2,459/2,459 trajectories have their rows
equal to their step list in order, summing to 30,809 = the row count.

The committed n50 runs sample **one step per problem** (stride 10, first step),
and that step belongs to exactly one trajectory. So each problem contributes
exactly one trajectory here and the independence assumption holds.

**This changes for the full run.** Design C (accepted) covers **764
trajectories over 155 problems** — ~5 per problem, strongly clustered. Any
interval computed there with n = 764 would be badly overconfident. Compute at the
problem level, or use a cluster-robust interval.

### What "trace valid" means here

The two axes are natively at different units: answer correctness is a property of
a **trajectory**, trace validity of a **step**. In this pilot:

> **"trace valid" = the sampled step's generated proof compiled.**
> It does **not** mean every step of the trajectory compiled.

The brief's framing of cell b — "every step verified and the conclusion is still
wrong" — is the *strict* reading and requires the full run. Under the
sampled-step reading, cell b is "the sampled step verified and the conclusion is
still wrong": weaker, but still a real result.

n = 50, at **both** temperatures. The two temperatures produce **identical**
grids (see §5 for why that is less surprising than it looks).

**The three rows are disjoint.** A vacuous pass is *removed* from the valid row,
not nested inside it, so all nine cells sum to n = 50. (An earlier draft labelled
the vacuous row "of which…", which wrongly implied nesting.)

## 3. Cell b — valid trace, wrong answer. All 8, hand-read

This is the cell the brief calls most informative, and it is the largest
off-diagonal cell: **8/50 = 16.0% [8.3–28.5]**.

| # | problem | level | ground truth | trajectory's answer | vacuity class | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | #5011 | L5 | 560 | 1287 | `6_contentful` | **genuine** — "there are 1287 different sequences of 15 coin tosses…". Wrong answer, contentful verified step. |
| 4 | #533 | L2 | 3214 | 4132 | `4_syntactic_tautology` | genuine wrong answer, but the verified step was a tautology. Extraction took `8` from the `=` chain; the trajectory's stated answer is 4132. Verdict unaffected (both ≠ 3214). |
| 6 | #5008 | L5 | 106 | 133 | `4_syntactic_tautology` | "The final answer is $m+n=1+132=133$." Wrong; verified step a tautology. |
| 8 | #493 | L3 | 4,989,600 | 2,498,760 | `5_ground_computation` | COMBINATION has 11 letters with three doubled pairs: 11!/(2!2!2!) = 4,989,600. Trajectory halves it. **Genuine** wrong answer. |
| 9 | #5061 | L5 | 630 | 49770 | `6_contentful` | **genuine** — wrong answer, contentful verified step. |
| 17 | #925 | L4 | 60 | 30 | `1_goal_is_True` | "Total number of possibilities = 3 × 10 = 30." Wrong. But the verified goal was literally `True` — the trace verdict asserts nothing. |
| 23 | #463 | L5 | 336 | 210 | `3_goal_restates_a_hypothesis` | wrong answer; verified step restates a hypothesis. |
| 29 | #757 | L2 | 16 | 17 | `5_ground_computation` | single-elimination with 17 teams needs 16 games. Trajectory says 17. Genuine wrong answer. |

**All 8 have genuinely wrong trajectory answers** — none is a checker artefact.
Extraction was exact on 7 of 8; on #533 it took the wrong numeral but reached the
same verdict.

### Exhaustive classification of all 8

An earlier draft said "2 contentful + 4 vacuous", which accounts for only 6.
The classes are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, and they sum to 8:

| class | n | samples | what the verified step demanded |
|---|---|---|---|
| `6_contentful` | **2** | 3, 9 | real content |
| `5_ground_computation` | **2** | 8, 29 | kernel arithmetic, no inference — **the two previously unaccounted for** |
| `4_syntactic_tautology` | 2 | 4, 6 | nothing (X = X after substitution) |
| `3_goal_restates_a_hypothesis` | 1 | 23 | nothing (the goal *is* a hypothesis) |
| `1_goal_is_True` | 1 | 17 | nothing (the goal is literally `True`) |
| **total** | **8** | | |

So the split is **2 contentful / 2 ground-computation / 4 contentless**. Class 5
is deliberately reported as its own row rather than folded into either bucket:
"the step was real arithmetic but required no reasoning" is a distinct claim from
both "the step asserted nothing" and "the step had content".

Reporting cell b as a flat 8 would overstate the case for step-level
verification being insufficient. The honest headline is:

> **2/50 = 4.0% [1.1–13.5] are "a contentful step verified and the conclusion is
> still wrong"; 2/50 are "a ground-computation step verified…"; and 4/50 are "a
> contentless step verified…", which says more about the formalisation than
> about verification.**

### Two of the 8 exposed holes in the vacuity ladder itself

Hand-reading the two `6_contentful` cases showed the classifier was wrong about
both, in two different ways — and a third hole was already logged during the
tactic-oracle work and never patched. All three are now fixed in
`tests/audit/vacuity_scan.py`; see §3a.

## 3a. Three holes in the vacuity ladder, found and patched

The vacuity probe ladder asks, rung by rung, how little a goal demands. A goal
that falls past every rung is called `6_contentful`. Three goals were reaching
that label while demanding nothing — the classifier's own logic says so, and the
rungs simply did not exist.

| # | hole | why it fell through every rung | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **`∃ x, x = e`** — logged during the tactic-oracle work, never patched | not `True`; not a hypothesis restatement; `rfl` cannot close an existential; `decide` cannot decide one over ℕ | the witness is handed to you by the goal itself |
| 2 | **conjunction of its own hypotheses** — found here, hand-reading cell b | `assumption` cannot close a conjunction, and `with_reducible rfl` cannot either | sample 3: goal `hh = 2 ∧ ht = 3 ∧ th = 4 ∧ tt = 5` against exactly those four hypotheses |
| 3 | **substitute-then-compute** — an internal inconsistency in the ladder | rung 4 already granted `subst_vars` for free (`P_substrfl`), but rung 5 offered only *bare* `rfl`/`decide`, which cannot fire while the goal still mentions variables pinned by hypotheses | sample 9: `Nat.choose (gold + silver) gold = 70` with `gold = 4`, `silver = 4` |

Hole 3 is worth stating plainly: it was not an oversight about an exotic goal
shape, it was **the ladder contradicting itself**. Substitution was free at rung
4 and not free at rung 5, so a goal demanding nothing but substitution followed
by kernel computation fell past both.

Five probes were added:

| probe | tactic | rung it serves |
|---|---|---|
| `P_exists_refl` | `exact ⟨_, rfl⟩` | new class `4b_exists_given_witness` |
| `P_conj_assum` | `and_intros <;> assumption` | 3 — goal restates hypotheses |
| `P_conj_substrfl` | `subst_vars <;> and_intros <;> with_reducible rfl` | 4 — syntactic tautology |
| `P_subst_rfl` | `subst_vars <;> rfl` | 5 — ground computation |
| `P_subst_decide` | `subst_vars <;> decide` | 5 — ground computation |

Every existing probe, class name and meaning is unchanged, so committed figures
stay comparable; classes 3 and 4 are extended to their conjunctive forms, class 5
gains the substitution it should always have had, and exactly one class is new.

**Each new probe ships with a positive and a negative control**, checked in a
`PREFLIGHT` table before any real work, and the scan raises `SystemExit` rather
than emitting numbers from a probe that misbehaves. A probe never seen to fire is
not known to work; a probe never seen to stay *silent* is not known to be sound.
The negative controls are the load-bearing half — e.g. `P_exists_refl` must
**not** fire on `∃ a, a^2 = n` with `n = 64`, where the witness is genuinely not
handed over. See `docs/TESTING_NOTES.md`.

> **Status: the patch and its preflight are committed; the Lean re-run over the
> pass set was still executing when this document was written.** The re-run
> regenerates `results/vacuity_scan.json`, and through it `CONTENTLESS_STEPS.md`
> and `PASS_SET_AUDIT.md`; the before/after goes in `STATS_CHANGELOG.md`. Until
> it lands, every vacuity figure in this document is the **pre-patch**
> classification, and the direction of movement is known: samples 3 and 9 leave
> `6_contentful`, so **cell b's contentful count will fall from 2 toward 0** and
> the contentless share of the pass set will rise. The headline in §3 is
> therefore an *upper* bound on the strong claim, not a lower one.

## 4. Cell c — invalid trace, right answer

**10/50 = 20.0% [11.2–33.0]**, and it is *larger* than cell b. Twenty percent of
trajectories reach the correct final answer while the sampled step's Lean proof
does not compile.

The audit already on the record explains most of this: of the 13 failing
samples, 12 fail for reasons that are not verdicts on the model's reasoning
(false formal statements, unprovable statements, toolchain parse skew). A step
whose *formalisation* is broken tells you nothing about whether the underlying
reasoning was sound, and the natural-language trajectory can be perfectly correct
alongside it.

## 5. Temperature

| axis | discordant pairs | McNemar exact |
|---|---|---|
| trace validity | b = 1, c = 1 | p = 1.000 |
| answer correctness | b = 0, c = 0 | p = 1.000 |

Paired design, so McNemar, not a two-sample test.

**The answer axis is temperature-invariant by construction.** The trajectory is
read from the dataset; our model never generates it. Temperature cannot move it,
so b = c = 0 is *forced*, and its p = 1.000 is not evidence of a null effect —
there is no effect available to detect. Only the trace axis carries a real
temperature comparison, and there the result is the same null the repo has
already recorded elsewhere.

This is a limitation of the design as specified: **the two specified
temperatures can only ever move one of the two axes.**

## 6. Are the two axes actually independent, and how do we know?

Three separate mechanisms, and the gate was made to fire on purpose:

1. **Runtime assertion.** `assert_no_lean_verdict()` raises on any of 16 Lean
   verdict fields, including FormalStep's own `state`. Every entry point to the
   answer path calls it. A parametrised test asserts it raises on each field
   individually.
2. **Static import check.** An AST test asserts `answer_correctness.py` imports
   nothing from the Lean pipeline. Negative controls confirm it catches
   `import verifier` and `from config import …`, and flags the real
   `verifier.py`, while passing `answer_correctness.py`.
3. **Signature.** `score_trajectory(steps, ground_truth)` takes step texts and a
   ground truth — there is no parameter a verdict could arrive on. Pinned by a
   test.

**Empirically:** the grid has all four cells non-empty under both vacuity cuts,
and the association between the axes is ≈ 0 (§8). The original bug
(`answer_correct = trace_valid and not has_sorry`) forced cells b and c to be
identically empty; both are populated, at 16% and 20%.

## 7. What is not measured, and why

- **The full trajectory-level run.** Not done, deliberately. A strict validity
  axis needs a proof generated and verified for *every step of every sampled
  trajectory*: Design C's 9,065 steps × 2 temperatures = **18,130 step-proofs**.
  Lean and Mathlib are built on this machine and verification runs locally, but
  Goedel-Prover-SFT is 7B and the local GPU is an **8.19 GiB** RTX 4070 Laptop.
  The committed n50 runs ran on an **NVIDIA A10 with 22.07 GiB**, so ~22 GiB is
  the measured requirement — a commodity-GPU job, not an A100 one. Quantising
  would change the model and break comparability with every existing result in
  the repo. Costed in `results/RUN_PLAN_200.md`.
- **The strict reading of cell b** ("*every* step verified"). Same blocker.
- **Category stratification.** The five paper-Table-2 labels are not in the
  release (Phase 0 Q3) and regenerating them is one LLM call per row over 30,809
  rows.
- **Generalisation beyond one MATH subject.** All 500 problems are
  `counting_and_probability` (Phase 0 Q4), so nothing here supports a claim about
  MATH as a whole.
- **Whether a wrong final answer implies a wrong *step*.** Answer correctness is
  a property of the trajectory's conclusion. A trajectory can reach a wrong
  answer through individually sound steps plus one arithmetic slip elsewhere.
- **Multiplicity.** Six statistical tests are reported (2 Fisher × 2 vacuity
  cuts, 2 McNemar). At α = 0.05 that is ~0.3 expected false positives.
  **None is significant, so no correction changes any conclusion** — under
  Bonferroni (α = 0.0083) the picture is identical.

## 8. Plain answers

**1. Are the two axes actually independent, and how do we know?**

Yes — enforced by a runtime assertion on 16 Lean verdict fields, a static import
check, and a signature that admits no verdict; all three have been made to fail
on purpose against negative controls. Empirically both off-diagonal cells are
populated (16% and 20%), where the original bug forced both to zero.

**2. How many traces are valid but reach a wrong answer?**

**8/50 = 16.0% [8.3–28.5]** under the brief's vacuity rule. But only **2/50 =
4.0% [1.1–13.5]** are the strong version — a *contentful* step that verified while
the conclusion is wrong. Four of the eight verified a goal that asserted nothing.

**3. How many reach the right answer through an invalid trace?**

**10/50 = 20.0% [11.2–33.0]** — larger than cell b. Consistent with the existing
finding that 12 of 13 step failures are formalisation defects rather than
verdicts on the reasoning.

**4. Does trace validity predict answer correctness — and with what effect size?**

**No relationship is detectable, and the study cannot rule one out as large as
about ±26 points in either direction. This is an underpowered result, not a
null one.**

| cut | P(correct \| valid) | P(correct \| invalid) | risk difference | odds ratio | φ (95% bootstrap CI) | Fisher |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| contradictory-hypotheses | 28/36 = 77.8% [61.9–88.3] | 10/13 = 76.9% [49.7–91.8] | **+0.9 pp** [−25.8, +27.5] | 1.05 | **+0.009** [−0.255, +0.309] | p = 1.000 |
| contentless | 19/23 = 82.6% [62.9–93.0] | 10/13 = 76.9% [49.7–91.8] | **+5.7 pp** [−22.0, +33.3] | 1.43 | **+0.069** [−0.258, +0.408] | p = 0.686 |

φ intervals are percentile bootstraps, 20,000 resamples, seed 20260902 —
bootstrap rather than a closed form because cell d = 3 and the normal
approximation is not trustworthy there.

**The point estimates carry almost no information.** φ = +0.009 with an interval
of [−0.255, +0.309] is consistent with a moderate negative association, no
association, and a moderate positive one. Reading it as "no association" is
exactly the error this section is written to prevent.

### How underpowered — quantified

| | contradictory-hypotheses cut | contentless cut |
|---|---|---|
| n₁ (valid) / n₀ (invalid) | 36 / 13 | 23 / 13 |
| **power at the observed effect** | **3%** | **7%** |
| **minimum detectable effect at 80% power** | **not achievable at any effect size** | **not achievable at any effect size** |
| best power reachable, even at p₁ = 100% vs 76.9% | 75% | 64% |

With n₀ = 13 invalid traces, **80% power is unreachable no matter how large the
true effect is.** Even if *every* valid trace had a correct answer and the
invalid rate stayed at 76.9%, the test would reach only 75% power. There is no
effect size this pilot was capable of detecting at the conventional threshold.

What n would be needed, holding the observed 2.8:1 allocation and the 76.9%
baseline, at 80% power and α = 0.05 two-sided:

| to detect | n₀ | n₁ | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 pp | 16 | 44 | 60 |
| 20 pp | 25 | 69 | 94 |
| 15 pp | 57 | 158 | 215 |
| 10 pp | 151 | 418 | 569 |

The binding constraint is **n₀, the number of invalid traces** — at a 74%
validity rate, invalid traces arrive at roughly one per four problems. Balancing
the allocation would roughly halve the totals above; see
`results/RUN_PLAN_200.md` §5.

**What the pilot does establish** is that the two axes are measuring different
things — both off-diagonal cells are substantially populated, where the previous
design forced them to be empty. That is what the redesign was for. Whether a
small-to-moderate association exists is a question this pilot was never large
enough to answer, and saying so is not a hedge: it is the difference between
"we looked and found nothing" and "we could not have found it".
